Thalassophobia

A 1–4 player co-op horror diving game built in Unreal Engine 5. Players descend into the deep ocean to complete objectives and recover valuable artifacts, managing limited light, oxygen, and communication while increasingly dangerous sea creatures close in.

Overview

A survival loop built on visibility, equipment, and threat

Thalassophobia's core tension comes from what players can't see. Light is a resource, communication between divers is limited by depth and equipment, and every dive is a negotiation between how much you can carry out and how long you can survive down there. Designing that loop meant treating lighting, inventory, and threat escalation as connected systems rather than separate features.

Technical challenges

  • Co-op state under pressure. Objectives, inventory, and creature encounters all need to replicate correctly for up to 4 players without desyncing under packet loss or host migration.
  • Believable creature movement. Fish, multi-legged creatures, and tentacled horrors each need distinct, physically grounded locomotion rather than shared animation rigs.
  • Readable horror underwater. Visibility, lighting, and audio needed to create tension without becoming unplayable or disorienting.
  • Reusable foundations. Systems had to be built as modular components so future Varattu Valo projects could reuse them, not just this game.

Systems built

What's underneath the game

→

Multiplayer gameplay architecture

Authority-safe replication for shared inventory, objectives, and creature encounters across up to 4 players.

→

Modular gameplay framework

Ability- and component-driven architecture so new equipment and creature types plug in without touching core systems.

→

AI behavior systems

Behavior Tree-driven creature AI covering patrol, hunting, and reaction states across multiple creature archetypes.

→

Underwater interaction systems

Buoyancy-aware movement, equipment handling, and object interaction tuned for underwater traversal.

→

Control Rig & procedural animation

Procedural foot placement and creature locomotion — fish, multi-legged creatures, tentacles, and dynamic hit reactions.

→

Reusable engine plugins

Core gameplay, AI, and animation systems built as standalone plugins shared across Varattu Valo's projects.
